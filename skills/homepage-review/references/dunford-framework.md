# April Dunford's Positioning Framework
## The 5 Components of "So What?"

This framework is adapted from April Dunford's positioning methodology to evaluate whether a B2B SaaS homepage effectively communicates "So What?"—why a customer should care about this product.

---

## The Five Components

### 1. Competitive Alternatives
**The Question:** If this particular product didn't exist today, what would customers use instead?

**The Answer Could Be:**
- Direct competitors
- Category alternatives (e.g., "spreadsheets" vs. a specialized tool)
- Status quo / manual processes
- Internal builds

**What to Look For on Homepages:**
- Clear statement of what they're replacing
- Named alternative (specific competitor OR category)
- Description of the pain with the alternative

**Examples:**

**Weak:**
- "Unlike other tools..."
- "Better than alternatives..."

**Moderate:**
- "Unlike traditional survey builders..."
- "Replaces manual processes..."

**Strong:**
- "Unlike manual survey tools where you spend hours mapping conditional logic, Sprig's agent handles all branching automatically"
- "Instead of building surveys in Google Forms and spending hours on logic..."

**Note:** Naming specific competitors is optional. What matters is clarity of the comparative point.

---

### 2. Distinct Capabilities
**The Question:** What distinct capabilities does this product have that alternatives do not?

**What Makes It Distinct:**
- Not just features everyone has
- Not just "AI-powered" (too generic)
- Specific functionality or approach that's unique

**What to Look For on Homepages:**
- Explanation of HOW it works (process, mechanism)
- What specific manual work it removes
- Step-by-step transformation

**Examples:**

**Weak:**
- "AI design agent builds the study"
- "Automated survey creation"

**Moderate:**
- "AI design agent builds the study from your questionnaire"
- "Automatically transcribes and formats your voice"

**Strong:**
- "AI design agent reads your questionnaire, outlines the mapping logic, and automatically builds the study without manual setup"
- "Rambled thoughts become clear, perfectly formatted text without the filler words or typos"

**Grading Criteria:**
- Does it explain the PROCESS (not just the outcome)?
- Does it name the SPECIFIC manual work removed?
- Does it show the MECHANISM (how it achieves this)?

---

### 3. Differentiated Value
**The Question:** What value do these distinct capabilities enable for customers?

**What Makes Value Differentiated:**
- Not generic outcomes ("save time," "generate revenue")
- Specific transformation
- Quantified when possible
- Addresses a concern or barrier

**What to Look For on Homepages:**
- Before/after transformation
- Quantified outcomes
- Specific value unlocked by the capabilities

**Examples:**

**Weak:**
- "Save time on surveys"
- "Increase productivity"
- "Boost efficiency"

**Moderate:**
- "Move from question to insight faster"
- "Spend less time on manual work"

**Strong:**
- "Move from question to defensible insight in hours, not weeks, without compromising research rigor"
- "Save up to 90% of time and cost on video production"
- "Create studio-quality videos in 160+ languages"

**Grading Criteria:**
- Is it quantified? (hours vs. weeks, 90% savings)
- Does it address a concern? (maintains rigor, studio quality)
- Is it specific to this product's capabilities?

---

### 4. Best-Fit Accounts
**The Question:** What types of companies care a lot about that differentiated value?

**What Defines Best-Fit:**
- Company stage (startup, mid-market, enterprise)
- Team type (technical, go-to-market, data)
- Situation (scaling, migrating, consolidating)
- Industry or vertical

**What to Look For on Homepages:**
- Named customer segments
- Specific company characteristics
- Situational qualifiers

**Examples:**

**Weak:**
- "For growing companies"
- "For businesses of all sizes"
- "For modern teams"

**Moderate:**
- "For mid-market teams"
- "For B2B companies"
- "For technical teams"

**Strong:**
- "Enterprise teams moving from manual workflows to scaled research programs"
- "Data teams at Series B+ companies with complex analytics needs"
- "Go-to-market teams at product-led companies"

**Grading Criteria:**
- Is the segment specific enough that someone knows if they fit?
- Does it reference stage, size, or situation?
- Would non-fits self-select out?

---

### 5. Market Category
**The Question:** What market category context makes your differentiated value obvious for these best-fit accounts?

**What Category Provides:**
- Frame of reference
- Comparison set
- Buyer expectations

**What to Look For on Homepages:**
- Clear category placement
- Context that makes value obvious
- Subcategory or new category definition

**Examples:**

**Weak:**
- "Analytics platform"
- "Survey tool"
- "Video software"

**Moderate:**
- "AI analytics platform"
- "Enterprise survey platform"
- "Business video creation tool"

**Strong:**
- "AI analytics platform for collaborative data work" (clarifies the subcategory)
- "Survey automation powered by research agents" (new category definition)
- "Studio-quality videos in 160+ languages" (context makes value obvious for global teams)

**Grading Criteria:**
- Does the category help buyers understand what it is?
- Does it make the differentiated value more obvious?
- Does it create a new context or clarify a subcategory?

## Using This Framework for Homepage Evaluation

### The Waterfall (Ideal State)
Dunford's full framework treats these as a waterfall:
1. Competitive alternatives →
2. Distinct capabilities →
3. Differentiated value →
4. Best-fit accounts →
5. Market category

**In positioning work**, you go through all five in sequence.

**On homepages**, it's unrealistic to expect all five to be deeply addressed.

### The Narrative Pivot Adaptation

**For homepage evaluation:**
- A "So What?" section is STRONG if it addresses ANY of these components deeply
- Better to nail ONE component (Strong) than touch all FIVE shallowly (all Weak)
- Most effective homepages focus on 1-3 components

**Common Patterns:**

**Pattern 1: Competitive Alternatives + Distinct Capabilities**
"Unlike manual survey tools [alternative] where you spend hours mapping logic [pain], Sprig's agent reads your questionnaire and builds the study automatically [capability]"

**Pattern 2: Distinct Capabilities + Differentiated Value**
"Rambled thoughts [pain] become clear, perfectly formatted text [capability] in real-time [value]"

**Pattern 3: Differentiated Value + Best-Fit Accounts**
"Move from question to defensible insight in hours, not weeks [value], enabling enterprise teams [best-fit] to scale research without sacrificing rigor"

### Grading Logic

**For each component present, grade depth:**
- **Weak**: Mentioned but generic or vague
- **Moderate**: Clear but missing depth or specificity
- **Strong**: Specific, shows process/mechanism, clear contrast or transformation

**Overall "So What?" Grade:**
- If ANY component is Strong → Overall = Strong
- Include notes about other components
- Note what's missing (for recommendations)

**Philosophy:**
Depth beats breadth. One component done exceptionally well is more effective than five components done superficially.

## Examples Analyzed

### Wispr Flow
**Components Present:**
- **Differentiated Value (Strong)**: "Rambled thoughts become clear, perfectly formatted text" - shows specific transformation
- **Distinct Capabilities (Strong)**: "Flow transcribes and edits your voice instantly...without the filler words or typos" - explains the mechanism and specific manual work removed

**Overall:** Strong "So What?" (nails 2 components deeply)

### Synthesia
**Components Present:**
- **Differentiated Value (Strong)**: "Save up to 90% of time and cost on video production" - quantified outcome
- **Competitive Alternatives (Moderate)**: Implies vs. traditional video production but doesn't name it explicitly
- **Market Category (Strong)**: "Studio-quality videos in 160+ languages" - context makes value obvious for global teams

**Overall:** Strong "So What?" (1 strong + 1 strong, even with 1 moderate)

### Sprig
**Components Present:**
- **Competitive Alternatives (Moderate)**: "Traditional survey platforms rely on manual configuration" - names alternative but doesn't paint pain deeply
- **Distinct Capabilities (Strong)**: "Upload questions → design agent builds study → synthesis agent parses data" - shows clear process
- **Differentiated Value (Moderate)**: "Question to defensible insight" - transformation stated but not quantified
- **Best-Fit Accounts (Strong)**: "Enterprise teams" - clear segment

**Overall:** Strong "So What?" (2 components are strong, even with 2 moderate)
