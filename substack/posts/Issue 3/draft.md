## **the accidental homepage audit: what B2B SaaS gets wrong every time (and the 3 questions to fix it)**

I'm currently redesigning my website and am at the part where I need to lock in on my visual brand identity.



I am not a designer, so I've been going through curated startup directories like [topstartups.io](https://topstartups.io) and [startups.gallery](https://startups.gallery/) for inspiration.



About 20 websites in, I realized that I'd stopped looking at their web design and started evaluating their homepage copy (*I mean, I* am *a product marketer)*.



Even in 2026, it's clear that B2B tech startups still lean on AI to draft their website messaging and positioning, despite the rise in AI slop.



While I understand the reliance on AI, it leaves gaps with generic language that could apply to anyone. Unclear audiences buried halfway down the page. Missing differentiation because "AI-powered" and "AI-native" have now become table stakes and no one bothers to explain *what* their AI does differently.



So I wanted to share my framework with practical examples to show you if your website can score an A, or just looks like slop.

## **the three questions most homepages don't answer**

A good number of the homepages I looked at made the same mistake. Their hero sections sounded clever, but didn't actually say anything.


*\[insert image of Omni Analytics\]*



"Omni turns your data into a source of truth for AI, *so anyone can get answers they trust*."



This definitely speaks to individual contributors who struggle with trusting their own data sources, but my immediate next questions were, which data? What specific teams are meant to be using Omni Analytics or how?



Here's another website with a similar header copy.



\[*insert screenshots for Amplitude\]*



I quickly learned that this was a common problem for tech businesses in the BI industry (we'll dive in with another example later on).



When I initially review a homepage, I ask myself 3 simple questions.



1\. What exactly is this product or service?



Not just "we're an AI analytics platform." What does it do?



2\. Who is it for?



Your target buyer should be able to see themselves by the end of your homepage, if not in your hero section alone.



3\. Why should your target audience care?



Proof points and case studies are great, but they're the bare minimum if you've been established for a while. B2B buyers are already using ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and other AI research tools to compare your services with your competitors.



If they land on your website, you should have a dedicated section that articulates why they should care about your business.



Pinpoint the exact pain point your target audience faces and weave that into your copy. Comb through your customer feedback interviews and win/loss analyses to see what your buyers are already saying as a starting point.



Most websites answer none of these questions in the hero section. Maybe some by the end of the homepage.



That's why I'll be grading a few I came across to give you an idea of what a good B2B SaaS homepage should look like.

## **example 1: hex.tech — grade B**

Headline: 'The AI analytics platform for your whole team." This is nearly identical to Amplitude's rotating headline and is just as generic.



The subheadline says: "Finally — anyone in your business can get trusted data insights from advanced analytics to simple questions in one integrated platform."



This is still generic. I could replace 'Hex' with Amplitude, Tableau, or Looker and the sentence would still work.



There's no competitor differentiation.



### applying the framework



|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| What is it? | Hex is a data hub where everyone from your data analysts to your marketers can upload data to analyze, question, and action the information relative to their goals. |
| Who's it for?  | The hero says "your whole team." The rest of the homepage indicates it's for anyone from Sales to Customer Success to Data Science, however, many of the integration examples and testimonials skew more to engineers and data analytics. |
| Why should a customer care? | Hex does what many B2B SaaS businesses do which is to simply write out their features. But, their \*why\* is buried in their FAQ. Hex replaces multiple data notebooks, BI tools, and one-off Slack requests through a centralized, self-serve platform.   &#10;  &#10;Teams can get the answers to their questions on their own. |



### what works



They do eventually highlight what status quo looks like, "juggling separate notebooks, BI tools, and ad-hoc Slack requests," stating that they're not just a notebook or BI tool, but a unified platform replacing multiple tools.



### what can be better



Hex buried the answers to all three questions under generic language and AI buzzwords. The consolidation story, the thing that makes Hex valuable, is there, but you have to scroll through the page to find it.



If your homepage makes people work to figure out what you do, who you're for, and why they should care, you've lost them.



### the neera rewrite



If I were their product marketer, I'd lead with their primary buyer and their pain point.



Headline: Your data team shouldn't be a factory for ad-hoc requests.

Subheadline: Hex connects your existing data sources in one platform. Run the analysis once, let everyone from engineering to marketing self-serve effortlessly.



This version does three things the current hero doesn't. It names the specific audience (data teams), names the exact tools (notebooks, BI tools), and states the transformation (ship faster, stop duct-taping). It replaces 'AI-powered' with a concrete explanation. //need to redo since the subheadline was changed//



Don't tell me it's AI-powered. Tell me what the end result is and how it's better than the status quo.



Current grade: B. *But they have done great work with their web design.*

## **example 2: statsig.com — grade C**

First off, I really do love the headline: "Measure what ships. Ship what matters." It's clever and memorable.



But the subheadline, "Test how each release - from features to AI-powered experiences - lands with customers, so you can protect trust and optimize at the speed of AI" undermines it with buzzword-heavy copy (*what does "optimize at the speed of AI even mean"*).



It also fails to identify the primary buyer or the pain point Statsig aims to solve.



### applying the framework



|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| What is it? | A multi-tool platform used to build better products systematically. |
| Who's it for? | After going through the full homepage, Statsig looks to be primarily made for product managers, but can also be used by engineering, dev ops, and data science teams. |
| Why should a customer care? | Similar to Hex, Statsig lists out their features, but fails to connect anything to any pain points. There is no transformative moment on their homepage that highlights the "Statsig" difference vs maintaining status quo or working with their competitors. |



### what works



Aside from their headline, prospects can install the Statsig DSK to run their first experiment and test the tool out for themselves. In a time where AI can compare B2B products succinctly for your buyers, giving them the ability to test your service independently without a paywall will help set you apart from your competitors.



### what can be better



On top of a subheadline with too many AI buzzwords, there's no competitive framework or transformation moment. Nothing that speaks to the buyer that says, "and here's why you should switch to Statsig." This is a website with a strong foundation, but weak execution. They have the right ingredients, but didn't assemble them into a clear, buyer-specific message.



Current grade: C. *And their web design is mostly standard for a B2B website.*



### the neera rewrite



I'd actually leave their headline as is. It's memorable, but we can use the subheading to identify the primary buyer.



New subheadline: Product owners should be able to scale experiments without overloading engineering. Feature flags, A/B tests, and analytics, all in one platform. Validate ideas in days, not quarters.



By identifying the primary buyer in the subheadline, web visitors will instantly understand who this product is for. Now, I do understand the instant push for resistance. Statsig, Hex.tech, even your company, may feel like you target many ideal customer profiles (ICPs). And usually these are categorized by job titles. But when you go through your buyer cycles, you will find a recurring pattern between who actually starts the conversation with Sales, who the final decision maker is, and who actually ends up using your products.



These are often 3 different groups of people. So while it feels safer to address all 3 on your homepage, you need to focus on that primary buyer who feels the burn that your service solves. They're your internal champion that gets your foot in the door because they acutely feel and understand the burn your service solves.

## **example 3: semgrep.dev — grade A-**

"Code Security for Builders" semi-identifies the primary buyer, but it's supplemented with the subheadline that clearly speaks to someone with a developer/engineering background as it highlights catching and flagging vulnerabilities and unifying SAST, SCA, and secrets.

I'm not their target audience, so I don't expect to immediately understand their copy. But they've done the best job compared to the previous examples in speaking to their target market.



This is further validated by their logos section, "Loved by leading engineering teams." *See? I was in the right range.*



My favourite part about this homepage is that it has a dedicated Why Semgrep section.



It identifies their primary buyer and includes that transformation point we keep talking about, "giv\[ing\] security teams visibility, control, and confidence." A transformation point doesn't have to take up a whole section or sound bigger than what it really is.



It just needs to succinctly outline what your service does differently.



Are you replacing something? Making it better than the status quo? Offering something competitors don't?



I would have loved it if they included a video demo of a security developer using their product rather than a static image so their target audience could easily see how they could use Semgrep themselves, but no one's perfect.



### applying the framework



|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| What is it? | A security platform that scans the source code to identify security threats (vulnerabilities) and enforces guardrails as well as coding standards. You can create your own "rules" or use their AI-powered detection to identify bugs for you. |
| Who's it for? | Developers, CISOs, and AppSec Teams as labelled under their "Built For Every Role" section. |
| Why should a customer care? | It's a security system that goes beyond shrinking the backlog (pain point) and increasing engineering velocity (business impact). It learns as you code by creating "memories" of your decisions to prevent false positives in the future - future-proofing your code for security flags. |



### what works



Semgrep does a great job solving what most B2B SaaS businesses get stumped with. Speaking to too many ICPs at once. They easily resolve this with their "Built for every role" section that clearly labels each ICP and what value they'd individually derive from working with Semgrep. It'd be better if they moved this up higher, as the "builders" in their headline covers all 3 ICPs, but it's still helpful to have on the homepage.



### what can be better



The homepage is incredibly technical. It's clear that they know who they're speaking to, and who they're not. With that being said, for a buyer type like developers who don't have much patience for corporate jargon, leading your first feature callout with "Empower invention without friction," feels like a misstep. Does anyone really talk about empowering invention? If you're stumped for copy, look into your customer feedback and win/loss interviews to see what your own users are saying. Try, "Find real vulnerabilities, skip the false alarms."



Current grade: A-. *Design-wise, still basic for a B2B SaaS business. But it gets the job done.*



### the neera rewrite



While the subheadline helps to further clarify the primary buyer, it's a bit of a word jumble that overloads the working memory.



"Catch, flag, and fix real vulnerabilities before they ship, powered by security that learns as you build." This is in line with the rest of the homepage, but what does "powered by security" mean?



"Unify SAST, SCA, and secrets scanning into one platform built for today's software era." Calling out SAST and SCA helps to speak to the right target audience and reference what category their primary buyer is in, but again, what does "today's software era" mean?



New subheadline: Find real vulnerabilities, ignore the noise, fix before shipping. Unify SAST and SCA into one platform and see 80%+ fewer false positives with 2x faster remediation.



I kept the language that speaks to the category to ensure the target buyer sees themselves in the copy, while leaning on some of their best metrics. Developers particularly don't care for marketing fluff, so best to show them what you can do right from the start.

## **audit takeaways**

This turned out to be way longer than I anticipated. So if you made it until the end, I appreciate your time and hope you learned something to apply to your own B2B SaaS website.



Couple things to remember when drafting your homepage copy. Did you callout:

  - What your product/service does?
  - Who your customer/target audience is?
  - Why your customer should care?



And if you're stuck on copy, go through your call transcripts. There are tons of gold nuggets for copy inspiration lying right in front of you. When addressing your "why customers should care," always think about that transformative moment. Spell it out for your buyers (so ChatGPT and Gemini can pick it up when comparing you to your competitors).
