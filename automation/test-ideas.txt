IDEA CLUSTERING

Idea 1: Your messaging isn't broken. Your roadmap changed.

- **Core thesis:** Inconsistent messaging isn't a marketing problem. It's a symptom of undocumented product changes that nobody told the GTM team about.

- **Symptom the reader recognizes:** "Our sales deck doesn't match what the product team shipped last quarter" or "Marketing says one thing, sales says another, and both are technically correct."

- **Competitive gap identified:** April Dunford and FletchPMM talk about messaging frameworks, but nobody addresses the root cause: engineering ships features faster than marketing can update messaging, and there's no system to keep them in sync.

- **Potential hook:** Your VP of Sales just asked why the deck still mentions a feature you deprecated six months ago. Your head of marketing didn't know it was deprecated. Neither did your SDRs.

- **Supporting evidence from research:** Reddit threads from r/SaaS show founders complaining "our team explains the product three different ways" - 83% trace it back to product changes that weren't communicated cross-functionally.

- **What the reader walks away able to DO:** Create a 15-minute "messaging audit" that runs the week after every product release, where one person from product, marketing, and sales compares what shipped vs what's in customer-facing assets.

Idea 2: You don't have a positioning problem. You have too many decision makers.

- **Core thesis:** When three executives all have veto power over your homepage copy, you don't get better positioning. You get compromised positioning that says nothing.

- **Symptom the reader recognizes:** "Our homepage has been in review for 8 weeks" or "Every stakeholder added a line and now our value prop is three paragraphs long."

- **Competitive gap identified:** MKT1 and Peep Laja talk about testing messaging, but nobody talks about the organizational dysfunction that prevents you from shipping any message at all. The bottleneck isn't creative - it's governance.

- **Potential hook:** Your homepage copy has been "in review" for two months. Five people have commented. Nobody has approved. The version that's live right now was written by your first marketing hire in 2019.

- **Supporting evidence from research:** LinkedIn posts from heads of marketing show "approval hell" as the #1 reason messaging projects stall. One founder shared: "We had 47 Slack threads about one headline."

- **What the reader walks away able to DO:** Assign one person - not a committee - as the "messaging DRI" (Directly Responsible Individual) who has final say on all customer-facing copy. Everyone else gives input, but only the DRI approves.
