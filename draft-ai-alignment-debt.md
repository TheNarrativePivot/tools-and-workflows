# AI Won't Fix Your Messaging (It'll Just Help You Repeat It Faster)

I'm co-hosting a speed networking event for product marketers next month. Part of the intake asks what they're hoping to get out of it.

The same pattern keeps surfacing.

They're feeling the burn. AI has helped them produce content at record speed. One-pagers, pitch decks, email campaigns, thought leadership posts at 10x the volume. Some are churning out materials in hours that used to take days.

But when they look at pipeline, nothing has changed.

Deals are still stalling. Sales reps are still asking for something better. The volume went up but the results didn't follow.

If you're a founder, VP of Sales, or Head of Marketing at a Series B startup or later, you've probably noticed this too. Your marketing team is producing more than ever. But the content isn't translating into closed deals.

## speed masked the signal

We got caught up in how fast we could create content. The velocity felt like progress.

But that same speed masked the signal. Teams were producing too fast to measure whether the messaging was working. The volume made it harder to see what was landing and what wasn't.

AI didn't fix the problem. It just made it harder to see.

## what most teams are doing

If you've been following AI content marketing advice, you've probably heard some version of this: take all your internal documentation, customer transcripts, and product information and throw it into an LLM like Notebook LM. Have it parse the data and build out your messaging, your ICP, your positioning.

It sounds efficient. And it is fast.

But if your messaging and positioning haven't been validated with customers first, you're scaling unvalidated assumptions at 10x speed. Garbage in, garbage out.

I've heard this from product managers directly. They either barely speak with their product marketing counterparts (maybe once a month, or only when there's an urgent ask), or they're spending more time correcting the content PMMs produce because it's clear those PMMs are throwing existing documentation into AI without talking to the PMs first about the products they're trying to sell.

Some teams try to solve this by creating a sales enablement chatbot. Load all your docs into Notebook LM or a similar tool, give sales reps a place to ask questions in real time. But that has the same problem. Everyone asks questions differently. If the underlying content isn't accurate or specific, you're just making it easier to find bad answers.

## AI alignment debt

There's a term for this: AI alignment debt.

It's the gap between what your product does, what your team says it does, and what your customers think it does.

AI doesn't create this gap. It just scales it at a much faster rate.

Most teams never validated their messaging before AI came along. But slow content production forced some pressure testing. If it took days to create a one-pager, someone had to sign off on it. Someone had to review it. The slowness created checkpoints.

AI removed that friction. Now you can produce a deck in an hour. An email sequence in 30 minutes. A competitive battlecard by end of day. But if the underlying messaging is broken, all you're doing is repeating it faster.

## what you can do instead

This isn't about slowing down AI. It's about tightening the loop between what gets said in deals and what gets turned into content.

Most teams never validated their messaging before scaling it. Sales feedback doesn't consistently get fed back into marketing. Messaging isn't pressure tested with customers. But content production has never been faster.

If you're leading revenue or marketing, here's where to start.

### option A: push for real customer insights

The ideal path is to advocate for your marketing team to invest in win-loss conversations and customer interviews. These take time. They're not as fast as throwing content into AI and getting something back in minutes. But they surface the signal that content production can't.

The problem is that most product marketers are too busy producing content to do this work. Or it's just not prioritized because the results aren't immediate. But if you can create space for this, it's the most direct way to validate whether your messaging is working before you scale it with AI.

### option B: have your sales reps pitch internally

If customer interviews aren't happening yet, start here.

Have your sales reps pitch your product internally to your product marketing or product management team. Not from a deck. From memory. How they would pitch it on a call.

Listen for inconsistency. Hesitation. Unanswered questions. That's the raw signal your messaging should be built on before it gets scaled by AI.

At my last company, I sat with our sellers individually and had them walk me through their pitch for our latest product. Not the deck pitch. Their pitch.

Here's what I learned.

The sales reps were far more honest about how our product would make a difference for our target buyer than what was written in the deck. We sold an ad block recovery solution for publishers. The common objection we heard was that if a user downloaded an ad blocker, it was because they didn't want to see ads. And the publisher should respect that choice, not pursue ad block recovery.

But when I listened to how our reps handled that objection, they explained it differently.

They told publishers: most users don't download an ad blocker because of your site. They download it because they had a terrible ad experience somewhere else. But now they're blocking ads everywhere, including on sites with non-intrusive ad experiences. So you're losing revenue from users who probably never had an issue with your site to begin with.

Using a service like ours helps you recoup that revenue while keeping the ad experience user-friendly. The ad block user stays happy. The publisher stays happy.

That reframe addressed the core misconception publishers had about ad block recovery. But it wasn't in the deck. The deck showed why our service existed, who it helped, how to get started, what it cost. All the functional information was there. But the emotional insight—the reframe that made the sale—wasn't.

If I'd just thrown that deck into AI and asked it to build messaging, I would have scaled the functional pitch without the reframe. The thing that closed deals would have been left out.

## what this won't fix

Slowing down to validate your messaging will reveal that your messaging was always broken. It will force you to choose between speed and effectiveness.

This won't make content production faster. It will make it better.

And look, I get it. Your executive team is pushing for more AI adoption. Your competitors are shipping faster. The pressure to produce is real.

But AI won't fix a messaging problem. It will just help your team repeat it faster.
